# Emberveil Story Mode — Lore Primer

*This document grounds LLM-generated content (M-S20) in the canonical world. Every generated dialog node, encounter text, or NPC line must be consistent with what follows. When in doubt, match existing tone examples rather than inventing.*

---

## Part 1: The Three-Act Structure

### Act 1 — The Road and the Reckoning

The party arrives in the Emberwood region and discovers that the Ashen Veil has been operating longer and deeper than the Brightfall Council has admitted. The Act's primary arc traces the Veil's presence through rural encounters — cult road ambushes, corrupted shrines, suspicious graves — building toward the realization that the situation is not under control and has not been for years.

Key beats: Reaching Brightfall (may require negotiating the gate), meeting the Emberguard and learning its institutional degradation, discovering the cult's cell network in the Gloomridge, and making the first meaningful faction choice (Emberguard, Ancient Pact, Wanderers Guild, or neutrality). Act 1 ends at the Gloomridge border, with the cult's herald known and the Veilscar ahead.

**Act 1 biomes:** Emberwood, Stoneward, Gloomfen (Fen), Old Road, Gloomridge.

### Act 2 — The Scar and the Scholars

The party enters the Veilscar region and the world becomes more actively hostile. The Veil's operations are more organized here; the contamination is visible and the factions more desperate. Act 2 introduces the Library Ruins (and Marek Greel), the Plague Fen (and the Harvesters), the Ash Plains (and the bone-reader, and what the Plains used to be), and the Crossroads (and everyone who wants something from everyone else).

The Act's revelations: The Rift was opened deliberately. The Brightfall Council has been compromised. The patrol incident — which connects Orren's personal quest and Bram's backstory — was deliberate. The Sovereign is not the threat's origin; it is the mechanism. Something beyond the Rift is the actual actor.

**Act 2 biomes:** Veilscar, Plague Fen, Ash Plains, Library Ruins, Crossroads.

### Act 3 — The Gate and the Choice

The party reaches the Riftgate and breaches it. Act 3 is short in map geography and dense in encounter weight. The Veilspawn here are organized differently — they know why you're here. The Sovereign's approach involves a gauntlet through the Architect's Verge (which reveals the Rift was not an accident) and Ember Hollow (which contains the Pact's failing heartstone). The final approach to the Sovereign is the last meaningful choice: what to do with something that was a person once and is not quite anymore.

**Act 3 biomes:** Riftgate, Architect's Verge, Ember Hollow, Sovereign's Approach.

## Part 2: Faction Descriptions

### The Emberguard

Once Brightfall's sworn protectors of the ember-shrine network. Founded forty years ago by seventeen soldiers on a four-hundred-word binding oath. The oath has been revised eleven times. The current version is sixty-three words and omits the ember-shrines entirely. The Guard's institutional integrity has been progressively eroded by political pressure from the Brightfall Council, which has found the Guard more useful as a patrol force than a principled protective order.

Officers of principle resign or are pushed out. Captain Maer was the forty-third resignation under pressure; the previous forty-two are not well documented. The rank-and-file Guard members are not uniformly corrupt — most are doing what they believe is their job. The chain of command is the problem.

**Faction feel:** Authoritarian, procedural, internally divided. Interacting with the Emberguard means navigating institutional logic that exists alongside genuine soldiers trying to do something real. Allied actions gain road access and supply depot discounts; hostile actions bring patrols.

### The Ashen Veil

A cult organized around the Ember Rift and the entity half-through it. Not simply religious — a structured intelligence operation with genuine ideology. The rank-and-file believe, correctly, that the Veil exists and has been suppressed. They believe, incorrectly, that opening it fully produces liberation rather than absorption. The Herald knows better and doesn't care. The Sovereign experiences the question differently than either.

The Veil recruits from grief. Its entry ceremony (the Long Look) requires staring into a Rift aperture. Extended exposure degrades conventional language processing. Inner circle members speak a functional pidgin with seventeen grammatical anomalies that appear in no other human language.

**Faction feel:** Sincere, organized, wrong in a way they can't hear. Interacting with the Veil means navigating true beliefs about real things and false beliefs about what those things mean. Allied actions grant cult information and black market access; hostile actions trigger coordinated response.

### The Ancient Pact

Not an organization — a covenant between the settlers' descendants and the land's pre-human interest in its own survival. The Pact-stones are its infrastructure; the stone-monks, Thornwhisper Circle, and Elderwood hermits are its maintainers. Of the original thirty-seven Pact-stones, six are confirmed destroyed, eleven are Veil-held, and thirteen are unaccounted for.

The Pact operates at reduced capacity. The land still honors it, barely. Sacred sites without Pact-stones collapse into corrupted zones over time. Ember Hollow is what happens when a sacred site loses its stone entirely.

**Faction feel:** Ancient, slow-speaking, consequential in ways that only become apparent over time. Interacting with Pact representatives means honoring old forms and making real commitments. Allied actions unlock hidden paths and shrine restoration.

### The Thornwhisper Circle

Nominally neutral druid-scholars. Actually the most effective intelligence operation opposing the Ashen Veil. The Circle's public neutrality is a functional cover — every member is simultaneously a researcher and a field asset. Their published work is genuine scholarship; their unpublished work includes detailed Veil cell maps, a list of compromised officials, and a plan for coordinated action waiting for the right moment.

**Faction feel:** Precise, cautious, long-game. Interacting with the Circle means exchanging information of genuine value. They don't reward sentiment; they reward intelligence.

### The Plague Harvesters

Opportunists who built a profitable operation around Plague Fen contamination. They collect Veil-spore samples, living specimens, and contaminated human tissue. Their clients are diverse and not asked about. No ideology; a price list. The Harvesters know what they're doing is harmful and consider that a cost-of-business reality.

**Faction feel:** Mercenary, amoral, professionally competent. Interacting with the Harvesters is transactional. They don't judge. Neither should the party, if interacting with them.

### The Iron Covenant

A trade-city coalition enforcing law and taxation at the Crossroads. Bureaucratic, professional, entirely consistent. Tax everyone equally, enforce contracts without favoritism. This makes them functionally reliable and genuinely unpopular. Their current leadership is aware of Veil operations at the Crossroads and has not acted — whether from corruption or jurisdictional dispute is unresolved.

**Faction feel:** Rules-bound, precise, occasionally absurd. Interacting with the Covenant means navigating official processes that work as designed, including in situations where they probably shouldn't.

### The Brightfall Council

Nominal government of Brightfall. Nine seats, at least one Veil-compromised. The Council's public position opposes the Veil; their private actions have defunded every institution capable of opposing it. They opened the Rift thirty years ago believing it could be controlled. The people who made that decision are dead. The institution that made it is not.

**Faction feel:** Political, self-protective, formally correct in language that obscures functionally incorrect decisions. Interacting with the Council means working within a system that is partly broken without being able to say so officially.

### The Wanderers Guild

A travelers' trade cooperative that doubles as an intelligence network. Fixed rate schedule, dispute resolution process, sixty years of accumulated regional intelligence. The Guild is genuinely neutral at the member level; at the leadership level, it is concerned about Veil expansion for purely practical reasons. Bad for trade routes.

**Faction feel:** Collegial, self-interested, reliable within its scope. Interacting with the Guild means mutual benefit and information exchange. They'll help if it helps them.

### The Oathless

Former Emberguard soldiers discharged for refusing specific orders. Not an organization — a category. No leadership, no charter. Experienced fighters with no employer and no official status. Some fight for hire. Some have stopped. They share a quiet understanding that they were right to refuse and that this did not prevent the refused actions from occurring.

**Faction feel:** Principled, unorganized, tired. Interacting with the Oathless means meeting people who made the right call and are living with the consequences. They don't want to be heroes. They want their judgment to have mattered.

## Part 3: Named NPC Capsules (20)

### Captain Idris Maer
Forty-year-old Emberguard veteran turned Oathless. Twenty-two years of service, resigned rather than burn a civilian village. Tactical thinker. Carries the weight of forty-two predecessor resignations she has researched. Dry, professional, warm in moments she doesn't seem to plan for. Self-aware about her institutional loyalties and actively questioning them.

### Lyra Ashwalker
Late-twenties ranger, solo for two years tracking the cult cell that killed her mother. Scar along the jaw. Says the important thing and stops. Warms slowly; when she does, she's warm. Has a version of herself she describes as having died in a fen and a version that kept going. Still figuring out which one she is.

### Orren Gravetide
Quiet Emberguard soldier searching for where his brother died in the Veilscar. Buries the enemy dead after every fight. Seventeen personal vows. Has a cracked pauldron he won't repair because his brother cracked it. Understated humor. Stoic in the sense of actually processed his feelings, not suppressed them.

### Tessaly Veil
Rogue with twelve knives, a face-wrap, and a past working (unknowingly) for a Veil-aligned employer. Professional separateness as a default. Skills she won't explain. The name is her grandmother's. Will not be pushed on things she hasn't chosen to share, but does eventually choose to share them.

### Bram Coldfire
Scholar, forty-seven. Enchanted spectacles with a Veil-density lens. Seventeen shelves of notes from eight seconds of Rift exposure. Contributed to the patrol incident through a risk assessment that was technically accurate but used by people with bad intent. Has been making different choices since. Enthusiastic about alarming things. Very honest about his failures.

### Yasha Stonewill
Stone-monk. Calm as a practice, not an absence. Heals without being asked. Stops at every shrine. Seventeen hand-wrapping vows each morning. Her order lost their Pact-stone to the Veil seventeen years ago; she's been preparing to address that. Comfortable naming hard things plainly.

### Ash Prophet (The Herald's Voice)
Inner-circle Veil priest. True believer. Extended Rift exposure has modified his language processing — near-complete sentences occasionally drift into Veil-pidgin. Organizes rituals. Sincere about everything, which makes him more dangerous than a mercenary.

### Keeper Orvain (Veil Shrinekeeper)
One of the last active shrine-keepers, maintaining three shrines on a weekly unpaid circuit for eleven years after the Brightfall Council defunded the program. Practical about sacred things in the way of someone who has cleaned and repaired sacred things for decades. Partial face-veil is regional tradition.

### Hermit Lasse
Sixty years in the Emberwood, former cartographer, now a living archive of everything that has changed. Bark-robes that appear to be growing in place. A lantern that hasn't gone out in forty years. Slow sentence-starts that end somewhere unexpected. Excellent memory, very long perspective.

### The Herald
The Veil's operational commander. Eighteen years with the cult, forty-plus minutes of cumulative Long Look. Knows the Sovereign's actual goals and endorses them with clear-eyed commitment. Smooth, organized, persuasive without feeling manipulative until later.

### Sera Dolthwick (Brightfall Innkeeper)
Twenty-year Brightfall innkeeper and informal information broker. Knows everyone, has a filing system. Warm and efficient with the specific tiredness of someone who has heard everything. Information available at information-for-information rates.

### Scout Devra Voss
Guild road scout, fifteen years. Documents everything. Has been filing accurate alarming reports about Veil activity for three years. The reports have been received and not acted on. Professionally direct.

### Mira Ashbone (Bone Reader)
Itinerant elder who counts field markers across the ash plains, tends graves, and reads physical evidence of the pre-Veil past that most people miss. Flat affect. Delivers devastating observations in the tone of weather reports.

### Envoy Nessa Sorroway (Thornpact Envoy)
Thornwhisper Circle intelligence operative whose cover is herbalist courier. Knows more about Veil operations than most commanders. Guarded by default, opens to earned trust. Brief and precisely calibrated in what information she shares.

### Brand Holwick (Veil Convert)
Voluntary Veil convert, former fen-area farmer, lost his family in the first surge. Doesn't hide the brand. Explains his reasoning openly. Sincerely wrong in a way that is not stupid. Useful for understanding how the Veil recruits.

### Guide Rellan (Swamp Guide)
Fen-edge community member with partial plague-immunity from three generations of proximity. Guides travelers through the fen. Has a practical relationship with terrible things: they exist, you learn their patterns, you navigate. Slightly weary, not cynical.

### Jex Farren (Unusual Merchant)
Independent gray-market trader with current Guild membership and nondiscussed provenance. Amiable, deflects with humor. Not Veil-affiliated — they're bad for business. Will give exactly as much truth as he's decided you need.

### Warden Cassiel (Rift Warden)
Emberguard warden at the Veilscar boundary, past normal replacement schedule. Believes in the job even as the institution degrades. Has memorized every Veil patrol pattern through his checkpoint. Files the observations. Does not ask where they go.

### Ember Warden (Ancient Guardian)
Pre-Pact land-construct from before the settlers arrived. Was present at the original covenant. Communicates in old-form impressions rather than words. Interprets its charge very literally. Has not updated its threat model for the current epoch.

### Marek Greel
Former Library archivist, now independent map-seller. Left eye sees Veil density constantly. Signed the Rift suppression order and has been making different choices since. Seventeen shelves of notes. Academic, precise, honest about failure as a compensatory mechanism for a very large mistake.

## Part 4: Voice Guide

### The Emberveil Voice

The world is grim, the stakes are real, and the tone is: clipped, slightly mock-grim, occasionally self-aware. Not grimdark wallowing — more like a soldier who has seen enough to stop being surprised and not enough to stop caring. The good and the bad happen to people who mostly don't deserve either.

### Five DO-THIS Examples

**DO: Clipped, functional, slightly dry.**
"The gate is shut, traveler. State your business." *(Not: "Halt, intruder, who goes there on this dark night of ill omen!")*

**DO: Exposition through observation, not statement.**
"The mill hasn't run in three seasons. Wheel's jammed with something. Something that wriggles." *(Not: "This mill has been broken since the cult sabotaged the water supply three seasons ago.")*

**DO: Consequences rather than drama.**
"He takes your coin with a sneer and steps aside." *(Not: "He accepts your bribe, grinning wickedly as he pockets the gold, thinking himself victorious.")*

**DO: Quiet reveals over dramatic ones.**
"'My brother.' Three years." A pause. 'He's not in there. Just the armor.'" *(Not: "'THAT IS THE GRAVE OF MY BELOVED BROTHER ADRIC, TAKEN FROM ME BY THE CRUEL VEIL!'")*

**DO: Self-awareness that doesn't break tone.**
"'Courage — real or foolish, I can never tell.'" *(Light fourth-wall lean without acknowledging there is a fourth wall.)*

### Five DO-NOT-THIS Examples

**DON'T: Purple prose.**
*BAD:* "The ancient shrine pulsed with eldritch energies, its gnarled stones weeping crimson tears of forgotten sorrow."
*GOOD:* "The shrine-stone is black all the way through. Whatever it used to honor — it's gone."

**DON'T: Explained emotion.**
*BAD:* "She said sadly, grief written across every line of her weary face."
*GOOD:* "She doesn't cry. Her hands are very still on the document."

**DON'T: Unnecessary length.**
*BAD:* "You could attempt to use your diplomatic skills to persuade the guard that you have legitimate business at the gate and should be allowed through without further delay."
*GOOD:* "[Diplomacy - INT] Reason with him."

**DON'T: Faction characters without texture.**
*BAD:* "I am an Emberguard soldier. I follow my orders. All Emberguard are loyal and brave."
*GOOD:* "'Twenty years of the habit. I've stopped being embarrassed about it.'"

**DON'T: Stakes-announcement instead of stakes-showing.**
*BAD:* "This is your final chance to stop the Sovereign before all hope is lost forever!"
*GOOD:* "'It's open. I don't know how. I don't know when. I just know it wasn't yesterday.'"

## Part 5: World Glossary (35 terms)

**Emberveil** — The corruption spreading from the Ember Rift. Not a place; a condition. Pre-dates every human institution in the region.

**The Rift / Ember Rift** — The dimensional aperture opened thirty years ago. Located in what is now the Veilscar's center. Was opened deliberately by a faction within the Brightfall Council acting on fabricated intelligence.

**Veilscar** — The region of active Rift contamination extending from the Rift's center. Growing at approximately six miles per year. Will reach Brightfall in six to eight years if not closed.

**Veil-touched** — Partially modified by Rift exposure. Applies to animals, plants, humans, and structures. Not a single condition — a spectrum from 'slightly off' to 'functionally replaced.'

**Veilfire** — Rift-energy expressed as visible light. Purple-white. Not fire in the chemical sense; does not require oxygen or fuel. Damages things through a mechanism that physical chemistry does not fully explain.

**Pact-stone** — One of thirty-seven stones bonded to the Ancient Pact's network. Focuses and directs the land's natural protective energy. When active, holds Veil contamination at bay within its radius. When stolen or corrupted, can be redirected to serve the Rift instead.

**The Long Look** — The Ashen Veil's Rift-gazing initiation rite. Duration determines inner-circle rank. Extended exposure modifies language processing. Forty-plus minutes of cumulative exposure produces inner-circle alignment.

**Veil-pidgin** — The degraded language that emerges from extended Rift exposure. Functional, seventeen grammatical anomalies that appear in no other human tongue, three anomalies in nothing human-origin.

**Ashen** — Adjective for Veil contamination. Ashen soil cannot grow crops. Ashen wounds don't close normally. Something described as 'going ashen' is being actively consumed by Veil energy.

**Ember-shrine** — Sacred sites maintained by the Pact's keeper network. When active, they provide rest bonuses, slow Veil spread, and maintain waypoints in the regional network. The Brightfall Council defunded their maintenance eleven years ago.

**The Pact** — The Ancient Pact. A covenant, not an organization. The land's agreement to tolerate human presence in exchange for stewardship. Currently operating at reduced capacity due to Pact-stone losses.

**The Sovereign** — The Emberveil Sovereign. Was a scholar, once. Put on the crown that shows the Rift constantly. Forty years of exposure later, still human in syntax, not in the functional sense. The Veil's figurehead and the entity-beyond-the-Rift's instrument.

**The Crown** — A Rift-focusing lens shaped to fit the human head. Pre-dates recorded history. Concentrates sustained Rift exposure without immediate physical degradation. Trades cognitive death-prevention for gradual identity replacement.

**The Herald** — The Ashen Veil's operational commander. The inner circle's interface with field operations. Not the Sovereign.

**The Long Look's Grades** — Outer ring: minutes of exposure. Middle ring: hours over time. Inner ring: days of cumulative exposure. The Herald's circle: forty-plus minutes. The Sovereign: continuous exposure for forty years.

**Veilspawn** — Entities that have crossed through the Rift. Not from the world. Organized to the Rift's interest, not the Veil cult's. The cult uses them; the cult does not control them.

**Brightfall** — The largest city in the region. Seat of the Brightfall Council. Has a gate that closed for the first time in two hundred years three years ago.

**The Library Ruins** — Pre-Sovereign knowledge repository, burned eleven years ago. Still occupied by construct-guardians following their last order. The burning destroyed key Rift documentation, which was almost certainly intentional.

**Overwriting** — The Ashen Veil's technique for corrupting Pact-stones. Six months, eleven practitioners in rotating shifts. Shifts the stone's resonance from Pact-frequency to Rift-frequency using voluntary pain as the focusing lens.

**Pre-Sovereign** — The era before the Sovereign appeared and the Rift was opened. Approximately sixty years of relative stability under the Ancient Pact. The coffee shop on the Library's ground floor was reportedly very good.

**Ash Plains** — Former farmland turned contamination zone following the first Rift surge's heat pulse. Seven thousand people were farming it. Four thousand survived. The strain of wheat developed for the plains' clay soil is extinct.

**The Crossroads** — The only major transit intersection not inside Brightfall or the Veilscar. Controlled by the Iron Covenant. Factions converge here. The only place where everyone agrees not to start something.

**Elderwood** — The old-growth forest at the Emberwood's heart. Sacred site under the Pact. Predates human settlement. Has its own opinions about road proposals.

**The Stone-Monks** — Religious order that maintained Pact-stones for the Ancient Pact. Their order has been significantly reduced. Yasha Stonewill is one of the last active members.

**The Thornwhisper Circle** — Druid-scholar organization / intelligence network. Formally neutral; actually the most effective anti-Veil operation currently running.

**The Wanderers Guild** — Trade cooperative / intelligence network. Formally neutral; actually concerned about anything that disrupts trade routes.

**The Iron Covenant** — Trade-city coalition running the Crossroads. Formally neutral; actually running on three-hundred-year institutional momentum and a very consistent tax schedule.

**The Oathless** — Former Emberguard soldiers discharged for conscience. Informal category, not organization.

**Memory Shrine** — A specific type of shrine that stores echoes of the dead. The fen region has several, accumulated from the old war. The Ash Plains has many, from the burning.

**Veil-density** — A measure of Rift contamination per unit volume. Marek Greel's personal scale. Low readings (1-3): tolerable. 4-6: functionally compromised. 7-9: actively hostile. 10: off the scale. The Riftgate reads off the scale.

**Rift-shard** — Fragment of crystallized Veil-energy. Rare in Acts 1-2, more common approaching the Riftgate. Unstable if improperly handled. Extremely useful if properly handled.

**The Ember Hollow** — A biome where a Pact-stone site has gone completely dark. The land's natural regulation is absent; what fills the gap is Rift energy. The hollow is not actively hostile like the Veilscar — it's more like a very sick forest.

**The Architect's Verge** — Pre-human construction zone. The structures were grown, not built. No joints, no seams. The oldest inscription explicitly states the Rift was not an accident.

**Veil-lens / Veil Lens** — A device for perceiving Veil energy. The Seer gives one to the player in early encounters. Marek Greel's spectacles are a more sophisticated version.

**The Brightfall Council's Suppression** — Thirty years ago, the Council classified all documentation of the first Rift event. The suppression was itself documented, then suppressed again. Still technically in effect.
